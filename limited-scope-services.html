Limited scope services: get the help you need, handle the rest

Not every matter needs full representation. Limited scope services let you choose specific support, with the scope and fee agreed in writing.

What is limited scope representation?

With limited scope representation, a paralegal handles only the tasks you agree on, and you handle the rest of your matter yourself. It can lower costs and keep you in control, while still giving you professional guidance where it counts most. The agreed scope is confirmed in writing before work begins.

Examples of limited services

Legal advice and strategy

  • Reviewing your case and explaining your options
  • Helping you understand risk, strength and possible outcomes
  • Preparing you for court or tribunal appearances

Document preparation

  • Drafting or reviewing court forms
  • Assisting with affidavits, motions or written submissions
  • Reviewing contracts or legal documents before you sign, for matters within a paralegal’s permitted scope

Court and tribunal coaching

  • Explaining what to expect at your appearance
  • Helping you prepare what to say to a prosecutor, adjudicator or judge
  • Guiding you through procedures and timelines

One-time or single appearance

  • Attending court for a specific date only, such as an adjournment or an early resolution meeting
  • Speaking to the prosecutor or opposing party on your behalf for a limited, agreed purpose

How limited scope is different from full representation

Your responsibilities

  • Meet every deadline for the tasks that remain with you.
  • Keep track of court dates, filings and notices outside the agreed scope.
  • Share complete and accurate information and documents promptly.
  • Ask for help early if something changes, because scope can be widened by agreement.

Is limited representation right for you?

It is often a good fit if:

  • You are comfortable handling parts of your matter yourself.
  • Your matter is straightforward, or you only need help at one stage.
  • You want expert advice and a plan before deciding on next steps.
  • You want to control costs.

Full representation is usually the better choice if the matter is complex, the stakes are high, or you do not have the time or confidence to manage your side. A strategy consultation is a good way to find out which suits you.
