Step 1: Send your ticket

Use the free case review form to upload a photo of the ticket, or email it. Uploading is optional. If you do, the deadline and the charge can be checked before the review, so it starts with answers instead of questions.

Step 2: Free case review

You get a straight answer on three things: your deadline, what a conviction would mean for your points, insurance and licence, and your realistic options. If you want to proceed, you get a written fee and scope before any work begins.

Step 3: We build your defence

Disclosure is requested from the prosecution, and the officer’s notes and evidence are reviewed. The review looks for weak points, and for the best resolution to aim for, whether that is a reduced charge or a trial.

Step 4: We handle the legal process

The request for a meeting or trial is filed, the prosecutor is dealt with, and court appearances are handled for you where the court allows. You get updates at each stage, and the outcome is explained in plain language.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to upload my ticket?

No, it is optional. But a photo of the ticket lets your deadline and the exact charge be checked before your review, which makes it faster and more useful.

What happens in the free case review?

A short review confirms the charge, checks for conflicts, flags your deadline and explains your options. It is a screening review, not a full legal advice session.

When do I pay?

The free case review costs nothing. Fees are confirmed in writing before any work begins. See fees.

Will I need to go to court?

Often not. In many cases a paralegal can appear for you, though some hearings need you to attend. Many courts allow remote appearances. What applies to you is explained up front. See service area.
