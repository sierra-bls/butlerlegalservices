Careless Driving

Careless driving charge in Ontario

Careless driving carries demerit points, possible jail and a lasting effect on your insurance. Know your options before your first court date.

What careless driving means

Careless driving is driving without due care and attention, or without reasonable consideration for other road users. It is charged under section 130 of the Highway Traffic Act and often follows a collision.

Penalties on conviction

Careless drivingCausing bodily harm or death
Fine$400 to $2,000$2,000 to $50,000
JailUp to 6 monthsUp to 2 years
Demerit points66
Licence suspensionUp to 2 yearsUp to 5 years

Penalties are set by law and can change, so the current details are confirmed at intake. A conviction can also raise insurance premiums significantly.

Frequently asked questions

What are the penalties for careless driving in Ontario?

Careless driving under section 130 of the Highway Traffic Act carries a fine of $400 to $2,000, up to six months in jail, six demerit points and a licence suspension of up to two years. Careless driving causing bodily harm or death carries much heavier penalties.

Is careless driving a criminal offence?

No. It is a Highway Traffic Act offence, not a criminal charge, so a conviction does not create a criminal record. It can still have a major effect on your licence and insurance.

Will careless driving raise my insurance?

Very often, yes. A careless driving conviction can raise premiums significantly and may lead insurers to treat you as a higher-risk driver.

Do I need to go to court?

Careless driving is a more serious charge than a standard ticket, and court proceedings are usually involved. In many cases a paralegal can appear for you on court dates, but some hearings need you to attend.

Can a paralegal handle a careless driving charge?

Yes, careless driving is a Highway Traffic Act offence within a paralegal’s permitted scope. Where bodily harm or death is involved, the matter is often serious enough that you should consider a lawyer, and you will be told honestly.
