Stunt Driving

Stunt driving charge in Ontario

A stunt driving charge can mean an immediate licence suspension, an impounded vehicle and serious penalties. Early advice makes a difference.

Written and reviewed by Sierra Walker, Licensed Paralegal. Last updated October 30, 2026. General information only, not legal advice.

Act quickly. A roadside suspension and impoundment start immediately, and court dates follow. Reach out as soon as you can with the paperwork you were given.

What counts as stunt driving

  • 50 km/h or more over the limit where the posted limit is 80 km/h or higher
  • 40 km/h or more over the limit where the posted limit is under 80 km/h
  • Driving at 150 km/h or more
  • Racing and certain other dangerous driving behaviour

What can happen

  • At the roadside: a 30-day licence suspension and a 14-day vehicle impoundment
  • On conviction: a fine of $2,000 to $10,000, up to six months in jail, six demerit points, a long licence suspension, a mandatory driver improvement course and a major insurance impact

Penalties are set by law and can change, so the current details are confirmed when you get in touch.

How the service helps

  1. Review of the charge, the paperwork and the dates you must attend.
  2. Request and review of disclosure, including how the speed or conduct was recorded.
  3. A frank assessment of the risks, including whether the charge could be resolved on different terms.
  4. Negotiation with the prosecutor, or representation at trial.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as stunt driving in Ontario?

Driving 50 km/h or more over the limit on roads posted at 80 km/h or higher, 40 km/h or more over on roads posted under 80 km/h, or driving at 150 km/h or more. Racing and certain other dangerous driving behaviour can also be charged as stunt driving.

What happens at the roadside?

A stunt driving charge usually brings an immediate 30-day licence suspension and a 14-day vehicle impoundment, before you ever reach court.

What are the penalties on conviction?

A conviction can bring a fine of $2,000 to $10,000, up to six months in jail, six demerit points, a long licence suspension and a mandatory driver improvement course. Penalties are set by law and can change, so current details are confirmed at intake.

Can a paralegal handle a stunt driving charge?

Stunt driving is a Highway Traffic Act offence, which falls within a paralegal’s permitted scope. Where a matter is more serious or complex than a paralegal can properly take, you will be told and a lawyer will be recommended.

Will I have to go to court?

Stunt driving charges usually involve court proceedings. In many cases a paralegal can appear for you on court dates, but some hearings need you to attend. What applies to your matter is explained at the start.
