Ontario demerit points: the complete guide

What they are, how many you get for each offence, how many lead to a suspension, and how to keep them off your record.

What demerit points are and why they matter

Demerit points are part of Ontario’s system for tracking driving convictions. When you are convicted of certain driving offences, points are added to your driving record. They stay on your record for two years from the offence date. The points for each offence are set by O. Reg. 339/94 under the Highway Traffic Act.

They matter for three reasons:

  • Suspension. Enough points lead to warning letters and then a licence suspension.
  • Insurance. The convictions behind the points are what insurers look at, and they can raise premiums for years.
  • Novice drivers. New drivers have lower thresholds and additional consequences.

Points are added on conviction, not on being ticketed. If you fight a ticket and are not convicted, or are convicted of a different charge, the points can be different or none. That is the main reason to review a ticket before paying it.

What often has 0 demerit points

Ontario’s table lists only offences that carry points. Offences that are not on it generally carry none. Common examples:

  • Speeding 1 to 15 km/h over the limit
  • Speed camera and red light camera tickets, which go to the vehicle owner rather than the driver
  • Parking tickets and most municipal by-law tickets
  • Many equipment, plate and paperwork offences, such as failing to carry your licence

A zero-point ticket can still have a fine, a conviction and an insurance effect. And offences such as driving while suspended or without insurance are not in the table, yet are serious in other ways. Check the exact charge on your ticket.

When the point value can change

  • The charge changes. Points follow the offence you are convicted of. If a charge is reduced to a different offence, the points change.
  • Speed bands. Points depend on how far over the limit you were, and the band can depend on the posted speed.
  • Timing. Points are added at conviction, and count for two years from the offence date.
  • Other jurisdictions. Convictions in other Canadian provinces, New York and Michigan add points as if they happened in Ontario.
  • After a suspension. Once your licence is reinstated, points are reduced to 7 (full licence) or 4 (novice licence).
  • Amendments to the regulation. Point values follow O. Reg. 339/94 as currently in force, as published on ontario.ca.

If you accumulate too many points again after a suspension and reinstatement, your licence can be suspended for another six months.

How novice drivers face additional consequences

  • Lower thresholds. A 60-day suspension at 9 points.
  • Escalating penalties. Breaking graduated licensing rules, being convicted of an offence worth 4 or more points, or a court-ordered suspension for such an offence leads to a 30-day suspension for a first offence, 90 days for a second, and loss of the novice licence for a third. You then reapply and repeat the tests and fees.
  • Distracted driving. No demerit points, but suspensions of 30 days, 90 days and then cancellation.

See novice driver tickets.

How to check your demerit points in Ontario

  1. Order your 3-year driver record through ServiceOntario at ontario.ca/page/order-drivers-record. You need your licence number.
  2. An uncertified record ordered online costs $12 and arrives as a PDF by email. A certified record costs $18 and is mailed by the Ministry of Transportation, which can take up to 15 business days. The current fees are listed on that page.
  3. The 3-year driver record includes your demerit point total. The driver’s licence history does not.
  4. You can also order in person at a ServiceOntario centre.

Frequently asked questions

How many demerit points before my licence is suspended in Ontario?

For fully licensed drivers, 15 points means a 30-day suspension, with warning letters at 6 to 8 points and 9 to 14 points. For novice drivers (G1, G2, M1, M2), 9 points means a 60-day suspension, with warning letters at 2 to 5 points and 6 to 8 points.

How long do demerit points stay on my record?

Points stay on your record for two years from the offence date. They are added when you are convicted.

Do demerit points go away if I pay the ticket?

No. Paying a ticket is a guilty plea, and the points are added on conviction. Points drop off only after two years from the offence date.

Do tickets from other provinces count?

Convictions in other Canadian provinces and in New York and Michigan add points to your Ontario record as if the offence happened in Ontario.

Do demerit points affect my car insurance?

Insurers generally look at convictions and at-fault claims rather than your points total, but the offences that carry points are the ones that tend to raise premiums.

Which offences carry zero demerit points?

Speeding 1 to 15 km/h over carries none, and camera-based speed and red light tickets, parking tickets and many equipment or paperwork offences generally carry none. The ticket wording decides, so check it.

What happens to my points after a suspension?

After a suspension ends and your licence is reinstated, your points are reduced to 7 for a full licence or 4 for a novice licence, and those stay for two years. If you reach the limit again, the licence is suspended for 6 months.

How do I check my demerit points?

Order your 3-year driver record from ServiceOntario. It shows your demerit point total. The driver’s licence history does not.
